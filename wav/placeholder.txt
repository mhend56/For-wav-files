
The latest RNZ–Reid Research poll reveals an electorate that is fragmented, restless, and increasingly unconvinced by the major parties. Labour remains ahead on 32.2 percent, yet the lead is fragile; the party has not managed to convert public dissatisfaction into meaningful momentum. Despite weeks of internal instability, National’s 29.1 percent suggests a surprisingly durable base, with voters either loyal or disengaged enough to overlook recent turmoil. What stands out most in this poll is the Opportunity Party crossing the five‑percent threshold for the first time, instantly becoming a pivotal force in the political landscape. This shift is not a minor fluctuation but a structural change that reshapes coalition arithmetic and signals a deeper appetite for alternatives.
